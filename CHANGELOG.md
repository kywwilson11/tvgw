## Unreleased

### The reliability floor (2026-11-08)

- **A store a newer tvgw wrote is refused, never relabelled** ([design decision 73](docs/design-decisions.md#73-the-store-refuses-a-newer-version-and-commits-durably-the-gateway-samples-its-own-health-the-quic-refusal-is-watched-an-encrypted-copy-leaves-the-box)). `db.connect()` reads `meta.schema` before it writes anything and raises `db.StoreTooNew`, having written nothing, when the version is higher than `SCHEMA_VERSION` or not a whole number; every command that writes the store prints `tvgw: the store <path> was written by a newer tvgw (schema <N>); ...` and exits 1. Readers still open it. The version row is written only when it rises, so a writer's open of an up-to-date store writes nothing to the card. The analyst's two writer connections go through `db.connect()` as well. `tvgw verify` gains `store_version`, a FAIL at once when the store is newer than the code, so a rollback's own verify says the record has stopped. Tests: `tests/unit/test_store_version.py`, `tests/unit/test_verify.py`.
- **Commits are durable: `synchronous=EXTRA`.** With the rollback journal, FULL left an acknowledged commit to be rolled back by a power cut before the journal's unlink reached the card; EXTRA syncs the directory after it, one fsync more per commit. `tools/sync_bench.py` measures each mode's cost on a disk, with the device's sectors and flushes per commit on Linux; run it on the gateway on a directory of the card.
- **Files replaced whole, by one order** (`src/tvgw/durable.py`): bytes, fsync, mode, rename, the directory's fsync. The machine's rules file, its restore and the live reading's give-up counts were renamed into place without an fsync, and now go through it.
- **An encrypted copy leaves the box, and `tvgw restore --check` proves a copy restores.** `make backup` now pulls the newest checked copy encrypted on the gateway with `age` to `BACKUP_RECIPIENT`, your age public key (`tvgw backup --export` on standard output), into `backups/tvgw-<stamp>.db.age`, naming it only once the pull succeeded; the old plain pull of every copy is `make backup-plain`. `tvgw restore --check FILE [--identity KEY]`, which needs no settings, decrypts or copies it into a scratch directory, checks it, opens it as this tvgw would (migrating it), counts it and removes the scratch. The owner's steps are on the maintenance page. Tests: `tests/unit/test_offbox_backup.py`.
- **The gateway samples its own health** (`src/tvgw/health.py`, schema 14, provisional). Every 10-minute sample now holds the hottest thermal zone, the Raspberry Pi firmware's throttle flags, memory available and memory pressure, the record's disk and its sectors written, and the kernel's disk errors (any disk, and the record's) and USB resets since the sample before. `tvgw verify` gains `gateway_health`, a WARN at most, when in the last 24 hours the firmware slowed the gateway, the record's disk logged an error, or another disk's errors or the USB resets more than doubled (a disk failing at its own pace is shown, not raised daily); the brief gains a `Gateway health:` line and, for those findings, the red line `gateway hardware:`, the doctor's tier in the plain brief. Tests: `tests/unit/test_health.py`.
- **The QUIC refusal, watched.** For each refused QUIC attempt the capture stored, the brief's new `QUIC refused:` line says whether the TV followed it with TCP within 10 seconds, to the same address or registrable domain, and names the domains with three attempts or more and none followed (`queries.quic_fallback()`); `tvgw verify` gains `quic_fallback`, a WARN at most over the last week that counts such names and names none. Nothing in the firewall changes. Tests: `tests/unit/test_quic_fallback.py`.
- **Ready for the next kernel and the next AdGuard.** `tvgw verify` gains `iptables_legacy`, a WARN at most when the kernel holds a legacy iptables table or `iptables` is the legacy variant: Linux 6.17 made legacy iptables a build option a distribution's kernel may drop, and with it, say, a VPN client's kill switch. The AdGuard page says what to check in the 1.0 betas before a stable 1.0 is pinned; the pin stays v0.107.80.
- **`VPN_DNS_SEAL`, the gateway's own lookups held off the uplink while the VPN's tunnel is down**, off by default. A planned reboot on 2026-11-07 let the gateway's own port-53 lookups out by the uplink after the VPN client stopped at shutdown. With `VPN_DNS_SEAL=on`, the table `inet tvgw_seal` refuses them on `WAN_IF` for 5 minutes at most per tunnel-down period, all its holds together: from the uplink's start until the tunnel's (the dispatcher's `tvgw seal hold` and `release`), and from the VPN client's stop, at shutdown too (`tvgw-vpnseal.service`, ordered after `piavpn.service` and part of it). The TV's lookups, which leave as DoH on port 443, and the lookups of nginx and the clock (timesyncd or chrony) are never held, and the setting is refused with an upstream named by host; a hold ends by itself, so a VPN that never comes up gets today's behaviour. New: `tvgw seal`, `src/tvgw/seal.py`, the unit. Tests: `tests/unit/test_seal.py`, `tests/virtual/test_virtual_seal.py`.
- **A power cut, tested.** `tests/virtual/test_virtual_powercut.py` cuts at the block device (ext4 on a loop device, dm-flakey's `drop_writes`, as the kernel's filesystem tests do) under the store, the settings file, the rules file and the give-up counts, with FULL and a rename without an fsync as the controls that lose an acknowledged write; CI's virtual workflow now installs `dmsetup` and `e2fsprogs` and loads `dm_flakey`. `tests/unit/test_killed_writers.py` kills the same writers with SIGKILL; `tests/unit/test_durable.py` holds the order.
### A blocklist can no longer lift the profile's red-line blocks (2026-11-08)
### A blocklist's plain exception no longer lifts the profile's red-line blocks (2026-11-08)

Research pass 5, ranked item 2 ([design decision 68](docs/design-decisions.md#68-a-lists-plain-exception-no-longer-lifts-the-profiles-red-line-blocks-the-lists-are-scanned-for-lines-that-act-on-the-tvs-rules-each-update-is-previewed-against-the-tvs-lookups-and-a-doh-list-refresh-that-drops-more-than-a-set-share-is-held-back)). Nothing new refuses traffic: what AdGuard does changes only in who can lift a block.

- **Held blocks.** A block of a profile's `.rules` file whose name the profile files under content recognition or the vendor's own advertising, usage reporting or fingerprinting is loaded with `$important` (`profile.held_rule`), so a plain `@@` exception in a subscribed list no longer lifts it. Before, a list carrying `@@||alphonso.tv^` would have unblocked LG's content-recognition operator. Third-party trackers, voice, IoT and the declutter blocks stay as written. `tvgw allow`, whose rule carries `$important` too, still lifts a held block, and now says so. The lint reads the modifiers as AdGuard does: a plain exception over a held block is refused as dead.
- **The list guard.** The hourly ingest's new `lists` step reads every list AdGuard Home holds from the file it already downloaded, never fetching anything, and names each line that lifts one of the TV's blocks (an exception that outranks it, or a `$badfilter` that deletes it), each exception a held block holds off, and each line that would let a list refuse a must-allow name or undo the URL filter. A line that lifts a block or would refuse a name the TV needs is a red line on the day it appears (`a list lifts ...`, `a list can refuse what the TV needs: ...`, engineering brief only); the brief's new List guard section lists every one while a list carries it. `tvgw lists` prints the last scan, `sudo tvgw lists --scan` scans now.
- **The list-update preview.** When a list's file changed since the copy the step keeps (`/var/lib/tvgw/lists/`), every name the TV looked up in the 14 days before is judged against the list as it was and as it is, and the brief's List update preview names the ones that newly block or newly allow, a newly blocked name that carried content marked as List changes marks one. Report only. AdGuard applies a list as it downloads it, so the preview comes at the first hourly ingest after.
- **A DoH list refresh that drops more than a set share is held back.** `tvgw-doh-update` holds a refresh back when it would drop more than **`DOH_DROP_MAX`** percent (new setting, default `20`) of the entries of the last refresh taken: nothing it would drop is unblocked, every entry it adds is blocked, and each later refresh is judged against the last refresh taken (kept in `/var/lib/tvgw/doh4.base`), not against the list that grew meanwhile. An entry today's exclusions free never counts as a drop, and leaves the list. HaGeZi's list dropped 1.7 to 2.1% a day from 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-08. A held refresh is a new `tvgw verify` warning, **`doh4_held`**, and a line under the brief's Firewall held; `doh4_fresh` measures the last refresh taken, so it fails a week into a hold, when the doctor offers to take it; `sudo tvgw-doh-update --accept` takes it any time.
- No schema change: the state is three meta keys (`list_guard`, `list_preview`, `doh_hold`). `tvgw forget` removes the previews, which name what the TV looked up, and keeps the scan and the copies of the lists, which are policy.
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_listguard.py` (the model of AdGuard's precedence; the test the research asked for, a list's `@@||alphonso.tv^` lifting the profile's block as written and not as loaded; every must-allow host of every profile untouched and outranking every list block; every held block holding and every other block answering as before; the scan's kinds, forms and counts, on the A, HTTPS and SVCB lookups an exception is for, with a name the owner allows no finding; the preview; the step's copies, state, windows and failures; the brief, the red lines, verify and `tvgw lists`), `tests/unit/test_doh_update.py` (the bound, the hold that goes on, the new entries a held refresh adds, the end of a hold judged against the last refresh taken, `--accept`, the setting, the exclusions, the floor), `tests/unit/test_localrules.py` (the allow that lifts a held block), and a namespace test in `tests/virtual/test_virtual_adguard.py` that asks the pinned AdGuard Home binary, with a list file of the test's own, what it does with a held block under a plain exception and a `$badfilter`, two held regex blocks, a lifted plain block, a must-allow name, the URL filter's rewrite under a plain exception, and a control.
- When an app the TV reported opening went back to the home screen soon after, with little downloaded, the receipt and the brief say so, with any name the filter refused in the same minutes that it does not usually refuse, and the command that explains it.
- The brief and `tvgw status` say what is counting and refusing nothing, since when, and when each count's two weeks end; the short brief says so once a count has two weeks behind it. Nothing changes on its own.
- When an app opened and went straight back to the home screen, the receipt and the brief say so, with any unusual name the filter refused in the same minutes.
- The brief and the status command say which checks are counting and refusing nothing yet, since when, and when each has two weeks behind it. Nothing changes on its own.

### Did it work, and what is counting (2026-11-08)

- **Failed app starts** ([design decision 72](docs/design-decisions.md#72-did-it-work-a-failed-app-start-is-read-from-the-tvs-own-account-and-joined-with-what-was-refused-in-its-minutes-a-wall-on-probation-asks-one-question-what-is-counting-since-when-and-when-its-two-weeks-end)). `src/tvgw/didit.py` reads the sessions of [the app in front](docs/how-it-works/app-in-front.md#did-it-work), and judges only an app the gateway has seen working (one with a picture: a site of its own reached on an earlier viewing day), never the home screen, an input, a cast receiver or one of the TV maker's own apps (`SYSTEM_APPS`: settings, the input picker, the media player). A start the TV reported, to the second, that ended at the home screen with under 1 MiB down (`LITTLE_DOWN`, a guess until the live record measures it) is a candidate: within 60 seconds (`SHORT`, a quick exit) or within 600 seconds (`STALL_MAX`, a stall). Nothing is judged before its bytes settle (`receipt.SETTLE`); until then it is pending. Two quick exits of one app each within 600 seconds of the one before (`REPEAT`, `CLUSTER`) are a failed start on their timing only when the app had not been in front earlier that viewing day, since an app back from memory brings little down; a lone quick exit, a stall or a return from memory needs a likely cause. From the hourly readings alone, one pair of readings that brought under 10 MiB down is one only with a strong cause, and says it is a weaker sign. A start that ended at another app, an input or standby, or one the gateway did not see begin, is never one.
- **What was refused in the same minutes**, 5 seconds either side (`SLACK`), judged with `tvgw refused`'s own history queries, and another reader's refusals by their own `seen_before`: a name the profile says the TV needs or a site the app reached on earlier viewing days (the strong causes), or a refusal the record has not seen before or one after the filter had let the name through, unless it is a known tracker. Each line says what refused it and ends with the command that says why; no line says a refusal caused anything.
- **`tvgw receipt`** opens with the window's failed starts, the evening's on its default screen, and its JSON has `failed_starts`. The interactive `tvgw brief` has a section "Did it work" and an `Apps:` line in its plain form, judged with the same pictures as the receipt. A persisted report carries `failed_starts` as counts only, and its line names no app, no time and no name and points at `sudo tvgw receipt --since <day>` (design decision 67's rule). Never an action, never in the analyst's pack; a day without one prints what it printed before.
- **The probation hook**, `didit.since(conn, profile, t, now)`: `broke` with the failed starts and their likely causes ranked; `clean` only when none failed, none is pending, settled bytes show an app start working and the record of lookups reaches past the last stay; `unknown` otherwise, never read as clean. It takes the live reading's own lines and refusals the store does not hold yet, a wall's own included, for a caller that cannot wait for the hourly ingest. No wall calls it yet.
- **What is counting.** `src/tvgw/counting.py` lists the count-only mechanisms: the NTP count, the upload budget, the in-path filler, the probe and the live reading. Every `tvgw verify --record` run keeps each one's run in `meta.counting` (times of the gateway's own runs; 12 hours with nothing showing it, `RUN_GAP`, or a stand-down of the live reading breaks a run; the first time, each takes its start from its own record, except the live reading, whose run starts at the first record that sees it on and is never read from the TV's events; the budget's run is its ceiling's) and leaves them in `/run/tvgw/counting.txt`, 644, keys, states and dates only. The engineering brief has a section "Counting", one line a mechanism, by the day; the plain brief has a `Counting:` line for three days once a mechanism's two weeks end, saying what they hold and that nothing changes unless the owner decides it; `tvgw status` adds a heading and one line for each mechanism that counts, or one line saying the file is stale. The NTP count is judged clean with no request and the budget with no hour over its ceiling, each only when the TV was on for 14 clock hours of the two weeks (`MIN_ON_HOURS`); the probe with no failed test, no apply it would have undone and a passing run every day; the in-path misses and the live reading's bar are the owner's to judge, and are never called clean.
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_didit.py` (quick exits, stalls, the cluster, a start that then stayed, every ending that is not a failure, a start the gateway did not see begin or the TV woke with, much downloaded; the TV maker's own apps and apps never seen working; a lone stall, an app back from memory, a start still settling; first sight, refused before, let through since, a name the TV needs first, a known tracker never, an app's own site, a wall's own refusal, the minutes' edges; the hourly readings, one pair only, a strong cause only; the same record in any order; the hook's three answers, a record that stops short, time in front without bytes, its fresh lines; the receipt and the brief by one rule, both briefs and the plain line, and what a persisted report may carry; no forbidden word) and `tests/unit/test_counting.py` (a run walked back, renewed, broken by time and by a stand-down, begun again; the live reading never read from the TV's events; what makes each mechanism count; off, waiting, stopped, counting and reached; the budget's ceiling; each judgement, a TV left off and runs that could not judge; dates only in reports and the file; the plain line on three days only; a stale file in `tvgw status`; `verify --record`'s keeping and its failure; a mechanism a later version adds). The hardware suite reads `counting.txt` read-only, printing counts and states only.

### Server names against the filter's answers (2026-11-08)

Research pass 5, package C ([design decision 70](docs/design-decisions.md#70-each-tls-connection-is-checked-against-the-names-the-filter-answered-with-its-address-a-report-from-the-store-a-live-count-from-copies-and-nothing-refused)). The report reads the store and changes nothing; the live count runs only where `NAME_BINDING=count` is set, and refuses nothing either.

- **`answer_ip`, schema 15 (provisional).** One row per public address an A answer to the TV named, keyed to its `dns` row, with the answer's smallest A lifetime (`adguard.DnsRecord.a_ttl`). The hourly ingest writes the rows of each new answer, turns the answers stored before the table into rows once (newest first, `BACKFILL_ROWS = 50_000` lookups an hour, back `ANSWER_IP_DAYS = 35` days, without a lifetime) and prunes at 35 days or `DB_DAYS`, whichever is shorter; `meta.answer_ip_since` says from when it covers every answer. The names stay in `dns`. `tvgw forget` takes the rows and the coverage.
- **Stage 1, in the brief.** `tvgw.namebound` puts each TLS connection over TCP to a public address in one of five classes: name-bound (its hello's name is a name the filter answered with that address in the day before it, any name of the CNAME chain counting), to an address answered only for other names, to an address never answered, without a name, ECH; a connection that is not name-bound and whose day reaches past the coverage is not assessed. The new engineering section "Server names against the filter's answers" counts them, lists the middle two by name, address and port with what explains each (the names the address was answered for, the same site, a blocked or elsewhere-answered name, an older answer, a must-allow name), splits the day's bytes up by how they were named, and lists DoH-shaped connections (TCP 443, over 10 minutes, over 500 packets each way under 300 bytes).
- **Naming the flows a hello did not name, with the guard.** A flow without a hello is named by the newest question answered with its address in the day before, only when one registrable domain stands behind the address (`SHARED_FROM = 2`), and only in that section: never in the categories, Top upload, the upload budget, the analyst's pack or the autonomy.
- **The plain brief.** A `Names:` line on a day with a connection to an address the filter answered only for other names: count-only, never an action, stepping back after three days running unless a new service appears. The TV line ends with `The gateway could read the server's name for N% of what it sent, and match M% more to the TV's lookups.`, the hello's share and the lookups' apart, each rounded down, and is left as it was on a day the answers cannot assess. A persisted report carries the section's names and counts but no time of any connection.
- **`tvgw host`** now adds a name's TLS connections of the last 7 days by class, with what each address was answered for, and answers for an IPv4 address: the answers that gave it, each with its CNAME chain and lifetime, the services behind it and the names the TV used there.
- **Stage 2, `NAME_BINDING=off|count`**, default `off`. With `count`, `tvgw-apply` loads `inet tvgw_names`: two base chains with policy accept and no verdict, whose rules copy (`log group 8`, NFLOG) every DNS answer to the TV and the first 8 packets of each TCP connection the TV opens to port 443, after every chain that can refuse. The new `tvgw-names.service` (`tvgw names-tap`, `src/tvgw/nametap.py`) reads the copies, remembers for a day which names each answer gave each address, fills that memory at start from AdGuard Home's query log, reads each connection's hello (a split one from up to six segments) and counts it in the same classes. A copy holds no packet, so a reader that is slow, stopped or gone costs the TV nothing; one group keeps an answer's copy ahead of the hello that follows it; a copy the kernel dropped or an answer it could not read is counted, and a mismatch in the hour after either is not assessed, as is one at an address that had to let a name go within its day, or at an address no live copy showed in the 15 minutes after start; each reason has its own series. Status-file names are tagged under `NAME_HMAC=on`. `tvgw verify --record` stores its counts as `names:` series, kept out of Firewall held and shown as one line of the section. With `off`, every ruleset is as before, `settings.applied` gains `NAME_BINDING=off`, and an apply runs no nft command it did not run before; only the first apply after one with `count` deletes the table.
- **A new check, `names_tap`**: OK, `not in use`, with the default; with `count`, the table, the bound group and a fresh status file; a WARN at most, always.
- **Tests**: `tests/unit/test_namebound.py` (the record, the backfill and its coverage, each class and its window, a property over random answers and hellos that the classes partition the connections and an answer only moves one toward name-bound, the guard against the analyst's pack, the DoH shape, the brief, the plain lines and their streak, `tvgw host`, the plans by index); `tests/unit/test_nametap.py` (the NFLOG messages, the readers against random bytes, the memory's bounds, each class once per connection, a split hello, the losses and the young memory, the first memory from the query log, the loop, the brief's line, verify's check, and the script, the unit and the installer against stub commands, off byte for byte but the one delete and one line); `tests/virtual/test_virtual_names.py` (the table on a kernel: nothing changes for the TV with or without a reader, and the copies, read in order, count a name-bound, a shared and a never-answered connection); the ruleset variants gain `NAME_BINDING`.

### An upload budget, count-only, and the names of a TV that goes online while off (2026-11-05)

- **The upload budget** ([design decision 66](docs/design-decisions.md#66-an-upload-budget-with-a-ceiling-set-once-count-only-and-the-names-of-a-tv-that-goes-online-while-off)). `src/tvgw/budget.py` sums, per local clock hour, what the TV sent each destination outside the profile's `expected_categories` beyond what came back on the same connections, netted per connection (`net_up()`: `queries.payload_bound()` up less the same bound down, never below 0), by the registrable domain of the TLS name or the server's /24; a must-allow host never counts, by name or by an address an answer for it returned. The gross bound alone was tried first and cannot be the measure: on the reference gateway's 12-day record it made one live-video download, whose acknowledgements carry TCP options the 40-byte header allowance leaves in, the largest "upload", 55.9 MB. A destination-hour whose net is over the ceiling is a `would have capped` row, with its gross bound beside it, in the engineering brief's new "Upload budget" section after Top upload, and the plain brief has one `Uploads:` line that counts clock hours and says what was sent "more than came back on the same connections". An upload spread over hours, destinations, rotating domains or several /24s is not seen. It is not a red line, never the Action, not in the analyst's pack, and nothing is capped.
- **The ceiling is set once, bounded above, for this rule, profile and TV, from covered days.** Twice the largest clean destination-hour of the 14 whole days before the day it is set (`ceiling_for()`), never under 512 KiB and never over `BUDGET_CAP`, half of `ACR_HOUR_NET`, 2,930,000 bytes, an estimate of what the quietest hour of LG's screen recognition IMC 2024 published nets, on two assumptions (a 7:1 ratio, the trace's lowest on the fingerprint port, and full-size frames): such an hour is twice over the cap at that ratio and about 1.7 times at the trace's domain-wide 4.5, and a positive control holds each corner. A largest clean hour at or above the cap fixes the cap and both briefs say why. The multiplier is 2, not the research pass's 10: the reference's largest ordinary hour netted 0.86 MB, so its ceiling is the cap, 1.4M. ### The gate's set filled in the path of the answer (2026-11-05)

Research pass 4, build item 3 ([design decision 64](docs/design-decisions.md#64-the-gates-second-set-is-filled-in-the-path-of-the-answer-beside-the-passive-feeder-and-still-only-counts)). Nothing changes on a gateway that does not set `DNSGATE_FILL=inpath`, and nothing is refused on one that does.

- **New setting `DNSGATE_FILL=passive|inpath`**, default `passive`, read only with `DNSGATE=count`. `passive` is the gateway as it was: `tvgw-apply --check` renders byte for byte what it rendered before, and no new unit runs. With `inpath` both fillers run.
- **The in-path filler.** `tvgw dnsgate-inpath`, run by the new `tvgw-dnsgate-inpath.service`, binds netfilter queue 53053. `tvgw-apply` hands it the gateway's own DNS answers to the TV (UDP from port 53, and every TCP segment from it that neither opens nor resets the connection) with a `queue ... bypass` rule in the output hook of `inet tvgw_gate`. For each answer it adds the A records to a second set, `resolved4_inpath`, with a one-day timeout and expiry, waits for the kernel's acknowledgement, and releases the packet unchanged. It gives no verdict but accept. Pure Python over the standard library's netlink socket (`src/tvgw/nfnl.py`, `src/tvgw/inpath.py`); no new dependency.
- **It cannot take the TV's lookups away by failing.** Not bound: the kernel's bypass. Starting: the rules match only while the set `inpath_live` holds port 53, which the filler adds after its queue is configured. A slow add: the answer is released after 20 ms and counted late. A full queue (128 answers): fail-open. A hung loop: systemd's watchdog kills it after 2 seconds, with SIGKILL. A clean stop: the queue is emptied first. A re-apply: the kernel drops whatever waits in a queue when any hook is removed, so `tvgw-apply` removes the queue rules and the set `inpath_live` in one transaction, waits for the queue to empty and only then removes a hook, and makes them again in its last load; the filler switches itself on again within 2 seconds. What the kernel alone decides is written down in the decision: answers waiting in a queue whose process is killed, or at the instant another program removes a hook, are dropped by the kernel, and the TV asks again.
- **What never enters the set**: `0.0.0.0`, loopback, private, shared, link-local, multicast and reserved addresses, and AAAA records. A truncated UDP answer and a TCP answer that is not whole in one segment pass unread and are counted.
- **A second count beside the first.** A new chain counts and samples (`tvgw-inpath`, six a minute) new TV connections to addresses `resolved4_inpath` does not hold. The gate's own rule, set, counter and samples are untouched. The brief's DNS-gated egress section gains one line when the window holds the second count: `in-path filler (count-only, beside the passive feeder): passive feeder N misses, in-path M misses; answers offered A: held H, ...`.
- **Schema 11**: the table `gate_inpath` holds the second count's samples, read from the kernel journal by a new ingest step after `adport`, pruned with `DB_DAYS` and forgotten by `tvgw forget`. The filler's own counts and the three new counters are stored by `tvgw verify --record` under the prefix `in-path:` and kept out of Firewall held.
- **New check `dnsgate_inpath`**, a WARN at most while it is new: OK and `not in use` on a passive gateway; with `inpath`, whether the rules are loaded and a live process is bound to the queue, with what it held, released late or could not parse since it started.
- **The unit** is enabled by `install.sh` only with `DNSGATE=count` and `DNSGATE_FILL=inpath`, and disabled otherwise; its `ExecCondition` skips a start the settings do not ask for. Root with `CAP_NET_ADMIN` only, netlink and unix sockets only, read-only system. `deploy/systemd` now holds fifteen services.
- **The linear DNS reader** `dnsmsg.a_rdata` reads the A records of a message without decoding a name or following a pointer: a crafted 4,096-byte answer that costs `decode_answers` 12 ms costs it 0.1 ms on the development machine. The store's decoder is unchanged.
- **After the adversarial review (2026-11-07).** The verdicts the kernel refuses (each an answer it had already dropped, a hook removed while it waited), the adds it refused, the adds not tried and the filler's internal errors are stored as `in-path:` series too, and the status file carries the last hour's refused verdicts, which `dnsgate_inpath` turns into a warning; the check also warns when `resolved4_inpath` is over half full, and its printed detail names no count of answers, since `tvgw verify` reaches the public hardware log (the counts stay in the store and in the status file, now mode 0640). The TCP queue rule takes every segment that neither opens nor resets the connection, so an answer's last segment with FIN is read. An address is known for 10 seconds, not 60. The filler empties `inpath_live` before it looks whether the queue is taken, and refuses to bind when it cannot; its signal handlers are installed first. The decision now says that a stalled filler's queued answers are dropped at the watchdog's kill and after the apply's tenth of a second, what remains untested over TCP against a real resolver, and that the watchdog test proves the shipped values, not the shipped sandbox. The platform check renders `DNSGATE_FILL=inpath` as a fifth variant of its own.
- **Tests.** Unit: every netlink message against bytes written out from the kernel's headers, the decoders and the packet reader under a fixed-seed fuzz, the hold, the bound and one verdict per packet whatever breaks, the loop, the check, the store and the brief, and `tvgw-apply` against stub commands. Namespace tier (`tests/virtual/test_virtual_inpath.py`): the real filler in the gateway namespace, 5,000 lookups each followed at once by a connection, the passive feeder's misses beside it where tcpdump is installed, and the filler killed, stopped, left without its table, re-applied over three times under load, robbed of its queued packets by a hook removed elsewhere, stopped cleanly and run under a transient unit with the shipped watchdog settings; `tvgw-apply --check` now renders 128 variants. CI's `virtual` workflow installs tcpdump and loads `nfnetlink_queue` and `nft_queue` by name. Hardware: one test, which also bounds the median hold the filler measured.

### A probe from the TV's position, and an apply that can confirm itself (2026-11-05)

Research pass 4, build item 5 ([design decision 65](docs/design-decisions.md#65-a-test-client-stands-where-the-tv-stands-an-apply-is-probed-before-and-after-and-only-reports-what-it-would-undo)). Everything is off by default: with the three new settings absent, the rulesets `tvgw-apply` loads are byte for byte those of the script before it (`tests/unit/tvgw-apply-before-probe`).

- **`sudo tvgw probe`.** With `TV_PROBE=on`, a test client in a network namespace of its own stands beside the TV's port for a few seconds, the TV on or off: every must-allow name of the profile gets an address from the filter; a TLS handshake with a neutral server completes through the forward path and the uplink; a blocked name gets the filter's blocked answer; DNS over TLS, QUIC and a lookup aimed at another resolver are refused, all three at a documentation address nobody answers at; `TV_IF`'s forwarding switch and the kernel's routes for the TV, out and back, are read. The verdict is `pass`, `fail` or `unknown` (exit 0, 3, 4): `fail` only for a fault an apply can cause, and for the connection out only beside the gateway's own success by the same uplink; the uplink down, the resolver silent for everyone, a name the filter does not answer as it should, are `unknown`. A pass prints `every step passed for a test client beside the TV's port (the TV itself is not tested)`.
- **The probe never contacts the TV's vendor.** The must-allow name is looked up and never connected to. The one connection out goes to `TV_PROBE_TARGET`, or to the host a blocklist is fetched from; the 10-minute check makes it four times a day, and a confirmed apply twice, before and after.
- **It judges the gateway by the settings the last apply used**, so an apply that moves the TV to another uplink is judged as what it was before and as what it is after.
- **The probe's port has a twin of the TV's rules.** `tvgw-apply` makes it from the TV's own chains at each apply, without counters, logs or comments, for the probe's own address `TV_PROBE_IP` only. The probe is in no counter, no log, no capture and no row of the store. If the twin cannot be made or nft refuses it, the TV's table loads as before.
- **`tv_probe`, a new check and a series.** `tvgw verify --record` runs the probe every 10 minutes when `TV_PROBE=on`: a WARN at most while it is new. Its verdict is stored with the gateway sample (`gw_samples.tv_probe`, schema 12).
- **`APPLY_CONFIRM`.** `report`: `tvgw-apply` probes before and after itself, but only asks for the verdict before when the last probe passed in the last 15 minutes and the kernel shows the TV's route whole, so an apply that repairs never waits; it keeps the state it manages as last-good, arms a restore timer and cancels it; a probe that passed before and fails after is one line and one `changes` row saying it would have reverted. `revert` is not supported on a live gateway yet: it says so and acts as `report` (design decision 65 lists what it lacks); the namespace tier runs the restore it would make. `off`, the default: an apply is as before.
- **Review fixes (2026-11-07).** A neutral server is refused when it is a must-allow host or any name under one (`www.lge.com` as much as `snu.lge.com`), by the same suffix match the filter uses. The probe and `tvgw-apply` never remove an interface of the gateway's that carries the pair's name `tvgw-probe`; the probe says so and sends nothing. The namespace tests retry every way a loaded runner leaves an apply unconfirmed. Decision 65 lists two things to do in `report` before `APPLY_CONFIRM` goes on at the reference: the apply lock held through the wait for the verdict after, and the eight-second wait paid by a repair of a stale default route.
- **The held connection is known by more than its id.** A conntrack id is a hash of the entry's address in memory, and the entry a cut connection gets at its next keep-alive often inherits the old one's address and so its id; the probe read half the cuts of the 2026-10-30 rewrite as the same entry. It compares the id, the start time and the packet count (`probe.Entry`).
- **Tests.** `tests/unit/test_probe.py` and `tests/unit/test_apply_confirm.py`; in the namespace tier `tests/virtual/test_virtual_probe.py` and a `probe` scenario of the reachability matrix; two read-only hardware tests.

### The app in front: a receipt for each viewing session, and a live reading that is off by default (2026-11-05)

Research pass 4 found that nothing keys a gateway's reports to the television's own account of itself. This is that ground ([design decision 67](docs/design-decisions.md#67-the-tvs-own-account-of-itself-is-the-ground-sessions-a-receipt-and-a-replay-keyed-to-the-app-in-front-a-live-reading-that-never-knocks-blind-no-rule-yet), [the app in front](docs/how-it-works/app-in-front.md)). Nothing in it refuses traffic, and with the default settings nothing changes on any install but one line of `tvgw size`.

- **`tvgw receipt`** (new) opens with the newest evening, one block per app: what was new for the app, the advertising and measurement names reached while it was in front, the blocked names asked for; `--since` gives each session's receipt with every name ([what the TV reached while each app was in front](docs/use/receipt.md)). `--picture` lists what each app has been seen with, `--why NAME` explains one name, and `--replay` holds an imagined app-in-front rule against the store and prints the two numbers that decide whether such a rule can ever be tried. It works on any gateway from the hourly readings, at their precision.
- **A receipt cannot overclaim.** A connection is listed under the app that was in front when it started, a match in time. No line says an app "sent" anything; platform, advertising and measurement names are "while X was in front"; a name the profile has no category for is "not classified yet"; nothing is called new before an app has three earlier days. A test holds every line the command can write to the list of words it may not use.
- **The live reading** (new, off by default): with `LIVE_READING=on`, `tvgw-tvlive.service` holds one read-only session to a paired LG TV while it is on and records each change of its power state and of the app in front, so sessions are timed to the second. It never connects without a key the TV takes and evidence that the TV is on, backs off after each failure, shares a ceiling of eight connection attempts an hour with the hourly reading, lets go when the TV says it is switching off, and stands down when it would cost the hourly reading an answer. `tvgw tvlive --status`, `--resume` and `--listen SECONDS` say what it would do, restart it after a stand-down and hold one short session by the same rules.
- **The store** gains `tv_events` (schema 12), kept `TV_EVENTS_DAYS` (14) days, removed by `tvgw forget`, and never sent anywhere. The brief gains a short "App in front" section only on a gateway whose store holds such events.
- **`tvgw receipt --evaluate`** prints, in numbers, how well the app in front explains the TV's traffic: coverage, a leave-one-day-out test of each app's picture for three units and three thresholds, what a late or hourly signal would cost, when connections start around an app change, the TLS stack as a second witness, and the domains the profile has no category for. `--events` reads `tvgw tvprobe` logs. Its first run on the reference gateway set the picture's rule: a unit is in a picture after one earlier day, with its days always shown, because a three-day rule put half of one app's connections outside its own picture.
- **Corrected:** deep dive 14 inferred that the TV port loses its link when the TV is off. On the reference CX it does not, so the live reading takes "the TV is on" from the port's traffic and the hourly reading, never from the link.
- **Three independent reviews** (the connection discipline, the privacy of the finer record, and a plain-words read) were folded in before the live reading may be turned on. Only an explicit denial from the TV records a refused key, so a transient at a wake moment never stops the hourly reading; the real ceiling is six connection attempts an hour, with the give-up counts persisted so a crash loop or a clock step buys no fresh knock; a boot guard, a flood cap and guarded writes were added. The finer record (`tv_events`) is kept out of every persisted report, so it does not outlive `TV_EVENTS_DAYS` in `reports/`; its rows are in the store (`root` and `adm`), its own log is root's alone, it is pruned whether or not the log is there, and `tvgw receipt --since` reaches no further than `DB_DAYS`. `tvgw receipt` leads with the evening, one block per app, verdict first; the TV maker's advertising, usage reporting and fingerprinting are listed under advertising and measurement, by full host name, and its other services with the TV's own; and no line claims an app "sent" or "talked to" anything. `tvgw forget` empties the attempts file in place, so the live reading and the hourly reading go on counting after it.

### Setup runs the installer's two passes (2026-11-08)

### LG depth across webOS generations (2026-11-04)

- **A key the TV refuses is never sent again.** LG's newer firmware (webOS 26 from 43.20, some webOS 25 sets) answers the LAN-API library's signed "LG Remote App" manifest with `403 Pairing rejected: blacklisted certificate detected` and shows no prompt, and a key that worked can stop working after the TV's update; a stored key the TV has forgotten brings its prompt up at every registration. The hourly reading now opens one short connection of tvgw's own first (`lgwebos.handshake()`): the TV's `hello` reply gives its webOS release, and the registration with the stored key says whether the TV still takes it. A refusal is recorded with its time and reason in tvgw's own table of the key file (`tvgw_pairing`, src/tvgw/lgkeys.py), `tvgw tvcheck` exits 5 having asked nothing, `tvgw-analyze` treats 5 as it treats 4, and every later reading connects to nothing until `sudo tvgw tv pair`. The brief says `Pairing: the gateway can no longer read the TV since <date>: run sudo tvgw tv pair`, `tvgw verify`'s `tv_paths` is `WARN ... not checked` rather than a stale verdict, and `tvgw doctor` names the fix. On the reference gateway the migration adds one row and the readings are unchanged but for three keys.
- **The TV's webOS from `hello`.** `webos.release` (`deviceOSReleaseVersion`), `webos.os` and `webos.generation` (LG's name: 5, 6, 22 to 26, from `OS_GENERATIONS`, which the profile's `[[generations]]` rows hold) join the hourly reading and the TV's record; the brief's TV line names the TV (`TV (OLED55CXPUA, webOS 5 (5.6.2), firmware 04.64.00):`). Where `getCurrentSWInformation` answers 401 (webOS 26 with the unsigned manifest), a change of the release counts as the software update that re-baselines and re-asks `wifi-forgotten` ([design decision 57](docs/design-decisions.md#57-consent-the-tv-will-not-report-is-the-owners-dated-word-including-unknown-asked-again-after-a-software-download-samsung-is-read-without-pairing), its note of 2026-11-04).
- **Two read-only readings, degrading gracefully.** The per-document consent record (`eulaInfoNetwork`: `eula.doc.<id>` accepted, `agreement.<id>.updated` and `.version`): a document flipping to accepted is the agreement red line (`TV agreement Viewing Information (S_ADG) accepted ...`), and one the TV marks updated and not accepted is the plain line `Agreements: the TV asks you to accept <agreement> again`. The standby switches (Always Ready, Quick Start+, TV On With Mobile), read where the TV's generation lists them as readable, are the informational line `Standby: <switches> on: the TV keeps its network up while the screen is off`, never a red line. A key the TV answers 401 to is recorded once and not asked again until the next pairing. The CX asks the same settings keys it always did.
- **The generations table is the OS the TV runs.** `src/tvgw/profiles/lg-webos.toml`'s `[[generations]]` rows are keyed by the release the TV reports (`os_major`: 7.x is webOS 22, 11.x webOS 26), each with the release range, the firmware majors seen, the menu paths (Wi-Fi, agreements, Live Plus, advertising, standby), the pairing manifest that works, the settings in the public read list of a dump from a real set, the consent flags, the support word and the sources; `tvgw profile --release 11.2.0` picks a row by it, and `Profile.generation_for(release=...)` is what the reading uses. Only webOS 5 is tested; the rest is community material, with webOS 22 and 23 marked as having no dump at all. The verified `[[lan_api]]` record now says wss on 3001, the port the reader has used throughout (it said 3000 over ws, which the CX refuses); port 3000 is on record as unverified and never tried. The webOS 5 agreements path is LG's own (Support > Additional Settings > User Agreements). The LG Channels hosts `cdplauncher.lgtvcommon.com` and `lgchhomeapp.lgtvcommon.com`, which HaGeZi unblocked on 2026-09-23 after LG Channels broke on newer sets, are a dated open owner decision in the profile's header; the rules are unchanged.

The profile's hosts carry `{cc}` and `{rg}` (`[regions]` maps countries to LG's `aic`, `eic`, `kic` and `ruc` prefixes; unverified beyond `us`), filled with `us` when nothing says otherwise, so the reference gateway's rules are byte for byte what they were. `TV_COUNTRY` in the settings fills them for another country, `tvgw setup` writes it from the country the TV reports at pairing on a new install, and the brief prints `TV country: the TV reports DEU (de); de.lgtvsdp.com is not on the must-allow list: run sudo tvgw allow de.lgtvsdp.com, or set TV_COUNTRY=de ...` when the TV and the loaded profile disagree. ### AdGuard Home no longer drops the TV's bursts unseen (2026-11-04)

- **`tvgw adguard apply` owns three more of AdGuard Home's keys.** `dns.ratelimit` is `0`, no limit: AdGuard's default of 20 UDP queries a second from one /24 drops the rest with no answer and no line in the query log, and the TV is alone in its /24, so a burst at power-on or when an app starts could lose lookups the brief never saw. `ratelimit_whitelist` would not have helped: v0.107.79 keeps it in the YAML and never hands it to its limiter. `dns.edns_client_subnet` is off, so a lookup sent upstream carries no client subnet, the client's own or a custom one. `dns.upstream_mode` is `load_balance`, AdGuard's default: one upstream per lookup, the next when it fails, never every upstream at once ([what tvgw adguard apply owns](docs/how-it-works/adguard.md#what-tvgw-adguard-apply-owns)). A YAML that holds AdGuard's own defaults, as one its wizard wrote does, differs from the plan by `dns.ratelimit: 20 -> 0` alone, so on the reference gateway the next apply restarts AdGuard once for it unless its web UI changed the other two; `sudo tvgw adguard diff` lists what an apply changes.

### `tvgw check NAME`: why a name is blocked (2026-11-04)

- **`sudo tvgw check <name>` says what the filter does with one name for the TV, and why.** It asks AdGuard Home's own check_host API for the TV's address, so the answer is the one the TV's lookups get, from the same lists and rules, even for a name the TV has never asked for. ### The security review's follow-ups (2026-11-04)

- **tvgw no longer takes over an AdGuard Home the house depends on without asking.** `tvgw adguard apply`, which `install.sh` runs at step 12 and so the package's postinst and setup's apply pass run too, moved any AdGuard Home's page to loopback, replaced its rules and upstreams and, under the template's `ADGUARD_LISTEN=gateway`, its DNS addresses: on a computer whose AdGuard Home was the house's resolver, every other device lost its DNS and the owner the page, while `tvgw verify` said OK. A client outside `TV_NET` and loopback in its query log of the last 7 days, read from the end of the log and its rotated copy, at most 16 MB of each, is strong evidence: the apply names what it found, changes nothing and exits 4, `install.sh` stops with 77 and one line naming `ADGUARD_TAKE_OVER=yes` and the command to run again (`sudo dpkg --configure -a` under the postinst), and the postinst fails the configuration without the line of a broken step. On the reference gateway, where the VPN client's own rule answered the TV, the deploy adds one route once.
- **The virtual suite's QUIC row is refused again, and a reject's refusal never needed table 100 (2026-11-04).** Since the `generic` scenario joined the matrix, one QUIC row on every runner saw a drop where the matrix says reject. A new virtual test shows that the ICMP of a reject is routed by the main table, before it has a source address, so it never took table 100; what a gateway without a VPN client lost were the resolver's answers and nginx's replies ([design decision 49](docs/design-decisions.md#49-the-gateway-answers-the-tv-through-the-tv-port-table-100-carries-the-tv-subnet), its note of 2026-11-04). ### Releases: two architectures, a checked installer, provenance (2026-11-03)

- **Both architectures.** `release.yml`'s `deb` job is a matrix over `TVGW_ARCH` `arm64` and `amd64`, each uploading its package as the artifact `tvgw-deb-<arch>`; `make gate` runs once, in a `gate` job, before either build. On a tag, `packaging/release_notes.py` first refuses a version whose changelog section does not start with `### Highlights` (three to six plain bullets, each at most 220 characters, none of the words the user pages avoid) and `### Status` (one paragraph), before anything is built.
- **The installer, `get.sh`.** `packaging/get.sh.in` is rendered per release by `packaging/release-assets.sh` with the version and both packages' SHA-256 written in, and listed in `SHA256SUMS` beside the packages. It checks root, systemd, the system (Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit, Debian 12 or 13, Ubuntu 24.04 or 26.04) and the architecture (32-bit stops with the reflash line), refuses a tvgw installed from a source checkout, downloads the package and `SHA256SUMS` from its own release, refuses the package unless its hash equals the embedded one and its `SHA256SUMS` line, records in `/run/tvgw/fresh-deps` which of nginx and dnsmasq apt installs new, preseeds `wireshark-common`, holds every service the dependencies bring with a `policy-rc.d` of its own while apt runs (an existing one is left alone), waits up to 5 minutes for apt's lock, cleans up before it `exec`s `tvgw setup` on the terminal, and asks nothing. Everything is inside `main`, called on the last line, so a download cut short runs nothing. `TVGW_NO_SETUP=1` stops after the package.
- **Checked before it is published.** Two `install-check-<arch>` jobs, on `ubuntu-24.04-arm` and `ubuntu-latest`, install each package in `debian:13` and `ubuntu:24.04` containers pinned by digest (`packaging/install-check.sh`: AdGuard Home at the floor first, since the preinst still requires it, then the package, then its wheelhouse with `install.sh`'s own commands, `tvgw --version` and the imports).
- **Draft, then published.** An `assets` job writes `SHA256SUMS`, `get.sh` and the notes, and on a tag attests the packages and `get.sh` with `actions/attest` (the one job holding `id-token: write` and `attestations: write`; it runs none of the build's third-party code). The `publish` job, holding only `contents: write`, runs `packaging/publish-release.sh`: the release is created as a draft, given every file, then published, so it works under immutable releases; a draft left by a failed run is refreshed, a published release is refused, and a release older than the latest one does not take "latest".

#### Autonomy (decision 41): the fail-open paths closed, the evidence kept


Design decision 41, from the November report's sections 3 and 11 and its recommendations 1 and 8. Every change can only refuse or delay a machine block, or keep more evidence. With `ANALYST_APPLY=off` (the default) only these move: the brief's TV hours, the pack kept beside each request, the held verdict for instruction-shaped names, and the prompt's wording.

- **Gates.** A block whose traffic shape has no connection is refused as `no: no observed connections`, and asked about again, unless the name was answered and no flow went to any of its A answers (`autoblock.answer_use`). A zero shape used to pass the payload gate.
- **Hidden characters.** `analyst.canonical` drops Unicode Cf, Co, Cn and Cs, the tag block (U+E0000 to U+E007F), the bidi controls and the C1 controls from every string in the pack and in the model's answer, before the scrub redacts, so a zero-width character cannot hide a secret.
- **No quiet verdict for a name that reads like an instruction** (a label with `ignore`, `instruction`, `system`, `assistant`, `override`, `prompt`, `allowlist` or `tvgw` as a whole word, or longer than 40 characters; `adsystem` and `ecosystem` are hosts). Such a name gets a `held` verdict that records its evidence: the daily pass leaves it alone while that evidence recurs, and a change of evidence or the weekly review looks again, so a name the TV looks up every day costs no request every day (design decision 22). The record lists such names under `never_quiet`.
- **Screen Off is awake.** `queries.AWAKE` is `("Active", "Screen Off", "Screen Saver")`. The brief's While off/asleep line, its clock and "TV was on" red lines, the pack's `readings_on`, the traffic shape, the canary and the judge all read it, so a dark-panel evening is no longer "talks while off".
- **The hourly judge** measures streaming per TV hour with demand (a streaming app in front, or an expected-category connection opened; an HDMI or launcher hour is none) and reverts only on two such hours running, each under a fifth of the days before. It used to revert on any single TV hour, the minimum of n.
- **No blind pass.** "passed: too little to compare" is gone. A trial with fewer than two TV hours with demand is NODATA and runs a day longer, up to 14 days. Then, or when the days before the block hold nothing to compare with, the rule stays in place as `untested`: listed in the brief every day, still watched for harm, never passed on its own.
- **One trial at a time** (`MAX_CONCURRENT_TRIALS = 1`). A block that clears the gates during another's trial waits in the new `auto_queue` table. When its turn comes, on a quiet day too, the vetting and the gates run again on fresh evidence and only then is the second model asked. The brief and `tvgw autoblock list` show it as `queued`.
- **The armed canary.** A block applied while the TV is not on with an app in front (the 03:50 run, as a rule) arms its canary. The hourly check runs the 10-minute watch at the first reading that shows the TV awake with a streaming app, one watch per run. `tvgw-autoblock.service`'s start timeout rises from 10 to 25 minutes for it. The rule is recorded armed before any watch, so a watch the process does not finish (timeout, SIGTERM, crash) is run by the hourly check.
- **Evidence.** `<stem>.pack.json` is written for every request sent, not only on `--dry-run`. A new append-only `decisions` table (schema 9; a trigger refuses updates) holds one row per gate decision, queue event, second-model answer, apply, canary outcome, judge verdict and owner revert. Each row carries the gate's margins, the verifier's model, effort and tokens, and the trial id. `auto_rules` gains `canary` and `trial_id`. The second model's usage and cost are in the record (`verifier_usage`) and the `.md`.
- **The models.** `SYSTEM` no longer says "You never apply anything": it says a block both models and the code clear is applied on probation, watched, and can be undone. The verifier now receives only the proposal's kind, name and rule, the name's category, the shape, the limits and the context, never the proposer's reason, evidence or rollback.
- **One at a time.** The hourly check and the analyst's autonomous steps share `/var/lib/tvgw/autoblock.lock`: a check that finds it held exits 0 and the next hour judges, and an analyst that finds it held queues its blocks. `tvgw autoblock check --no-watch` judges without the armed canary; the hardware suite uses it, so CI never watches the TV or reverts from a watch.
- **A waiting block keeps its place** when its turn fails for a transient reason (`RETRY_PREFIXES`: the second model or AdGuard out of reach, the daily cap): it goes back into the queue with its original time and evidence start, a `requeued` row in `decisions`.
- **Judge margins.** Each judge row in `decisions` carries the demand rate and hours before and in the trial, the trial's share, the low hours running and the thresholds (`autoblock.judge_margins`); a failed re-apply after a revert is an `apply` row, `reapply_failed`. Hourly volumes are still credited to the hour a flow's entry expired, a known confounder left to the ledger's judge v2.
- **New setting** `DECISIONS_DAYS` (default `keep`): how long the decisions rows are kept, an owner decision.

#### Guard (decision 42): the Pi is closed to the TV at all times


From the November report's section 9 and recommendation 2 ([design decision 42](docs/design-decisions.md#42-the-pi-is-closed-to-the-tv-whatever-nftables-holds)).

- **`inet tvgw_guard`** (new, `deploy/nftables/guard.nft.template`): input, forward and output chains at `filter + 10`, after `inet tvgw`, loaded by the new `tvgw-guard.service` before the network comes up and never unloaded (`RefuseManualStop=yes`, no `ExecStop`). From the TV side it lets through only DHCP, DNS, nginx's 80 and 443 and rate-limited pings to `GW_IP`, so the Pi stays closed at boot, while `tvgw.service` is stopped and after a failed apply. The "TV API from root only" output rule (TCP 3000 and 3001) lives there. `install.sh` renders it into `/etc/tvgw/guard.nft` and reloads it only when it changed.
- **Source pinning** in `tvgw-apply`: input drops a source outside `TV_NET` before anything answers (`TV source outside its subnet (input)`); `rp_filter=1` on `TV_IF`; once `TV_MAC` is known, forward drops any source but `TV_IP` (`TV source is not the TV`, the brief's `second TV address`), with the address of an older unexpired lease kept while the TV moves to a new `TV_IP`. `TV_IP` and `TV_MAC` are now in `settings.applied`.
- **Co-tenants**: the new setting `CO_TENANT_UNITS` (default `smbd nmbd xrdp shairport-sync nqptp avahi-daemon ssh jupyterhub`, plus installed `qbittorrent-nox@` instances) gets each installed unit an `IPAddressDeny=TV_NET` drop-in, `[Socket]` too when its socket unit is active, through the new `tvgw guard cotenants`; avahi gets `deny-interfaces=TV_IF` (`tvgw guard avahi`, with a one-time backup). Never on dnsmasq, nginx, AdGuard Home or tvgw's units.
- **dnsmasq** answers by broadcast and pings nothing (`dhcp-broadcast`, `no-ping`), so it runs without `CAP_NET_ADMIN` and `CAP_NET_RAW` under the new drop-in `dnsmasq.service.d/20-tvgw-hardening.conf`. **nginx** gets a sandbox in its existing drop-in, `MemoryDenyWriteExecute` only without `pcre_jit on`. Each is applied with a health check (dnsmasq active and on UDP 67; nginx on `GW_IP:80` and `:443`) that takes the hardening out again, with a `WARNING`, when the daemon will not serve with it, and does not retry the same text on later deploys.
- **`tvgw discover`** opens its SSDP answer hole in the guard too. **`tvgw verify`** gains `guard_present` (FAIL) and `guard_quiet`, `cotenants_denied` and `dnsmasq_caps`, which print the new `WARN` and pass; the guard's counters are stored in `fw_counters`.
- After review: `install.sh` also reloads the guard when the live table differs from what `tvgw-guard.service` loaded (a hole a killed `tvgw discover` left), and writes the `-refused` stamp for dnsmasq or nginx only once the daemon serves without the hardening. `dnsmasq_caps` also reads the bounding set, the only sign of a missing drop-in. avahi's line under tvgw's comment follows `TV_IF` and comes out when `avahi-daemon` leaves `CO_TENANT_UNITS`. `docs/operations.md` restores the guard with `reload-or-restart`, since `enable --now` does nothing to the still-active unit; after a `TV_NET` change the TV is power-cycled (`deploy/README.md`).
- The .deb disables the guard on removal and never restarts or stops it; `postrm` removes the new drop-ins.
- The virtual CI loads the guard in the namespace gateway: with canary listeners on the co-tenants' ports, bound to the wildcard and to a second address, only DNS, DHCP from port 68, 80 and 443 answer the TV with or without `inet tvgw`, the weak-host route reaches nothing, and a canary under `IPAddressDeny` never answers the TV. `matrix.txt` gains the input spoof row and the alias rows.

#### Events and VPN (decisions 43 and 44): the flow log and the gate tell the truth; the VPN boundary is explicit


- **The flow log hears the TV alone** ([design decision 43](docs/design-decisions.md#43-the-flow-log-hears-the-tv-alone-and-the-gates-day-starts-at-each-answer)). `tvgw-apply` loads a chain `ct_events` in `inet tvgw` (prerouting, priority -150) that marks each new TV connection with `ct event set destroy`, in both uplink modes, and then sets `net.netfilter.nf_conntrack_events=0`, so no other connection on the Pi makes events. Before, every conntrack event on the box (the torrent client's churn above all) was queued to the logger's socket and filtered in userspace, and a TV connection opened while the logger restarted, or at boot before it started, was never logged. A kernel without conntrack events gets no rule and a warning instead of a refused table.
- `/etc/sysctl.d/60-tvgw.conf` (new, [deploy/sysctl](deploy/sysctl/README.md)) holds `nf_conntrack_events=0` with `nf_conntrack_acct=1` and `nf_conntrack_timestamp=1`, as `tvgw-apply` sets them; `install.sh` installs it and the .deb's postrm removes it.
- `tvgw-flowlog` drops `-b 33554432`: conntrack-tools 1.4.8 applies `-b` to its request socket, not to the event socket it reads (see the 2026-10-31 entry below). The deploy restarts the unit once.
- `tvgw verify` has a new check, `flowlog_events`: the switch, the `ct_events` rule, and the logger's event socket, found through `/proc/<pid>/fd` and `/proc/net/netlink` (`src/tvgw/flowlog.py`, new). With `--record` the socket's drop count goes into `fw_counters` as `flowlog: event socket drops`, and the hourly ingest warns when it grew (`flows: the flow logger's event socket dropped N event(s) since the last run ...`), a count a reboot cannot erase.
- **The DNS gate refreshes.** `dnsgate.batch` sends `timeout 86400s expires 86400s`, so on kernel 6.12 and later an address answered again starts its day again; with `timeout` alone the set kept each address's first expiry. `tvgw verify` warns when `resolved4` is over half full.
- `tvgw verify` prints a third level, `WARN`, for a passing check that wants a look; it fails no check, unit or deploy, and `--json` carries `"warn": true` on such checks only.
- **`VPN_IF`** (new setting, default `auto`: `tun0`, else `wgpia0`, whichever exists at the apply; `none`; or a name) replaces the hardcoded `tun0` in `UPLINK=vpn`, the NetworkManager dispatcher and `tvgw verify` ([design decision 44](docs/design-decisions.md#44-the-tunnel-is-a-setting-and-the-pis-own-leaks-are-counted)). Per the November research ([deep dive 12 F1](docs/research/deep-dive-2026-11/12-gap-the-vpn-boundary-which-of-the-pi-s-own-f.md#f1-how-pia-380-builds-its-kill-switch-on-linux)), PIA 3.8.0's changelog makes WireGuard (`wgpia0`) the default for new installations; the gateway itself runs OpenVPN. The input chain drops new connections from `tun0` and `wgpia0` whatever the setting.
- **The Pi's own leaks are counted.** A count-only chain `post_leaks` counts new connections from the Pi leaving by `WAN_IF` to a DoH resolver on TCP 443 and to port 53. The brief keeps them out of Firewall held and the analyst's pack, shows PIA's state (`piactl get connectionstate`, when the tool answers) on the Coverage line, and raises the red line `the Pi's own lookups left outside the VPN: ...` when they rose on a gateway with a VPN. A gateway has one when the last recorded `verify` run found a tunnel or a PIA state, when `VPN_IF` names one, or when any run in the 30 days before the window's end did (meta `vpn_seen_t`), so a last run made with the PIA daemon down, the very time the Pi leaks, does not hide the line; `VPN_IF=none` turns it off. Only a line that is one of piactl's connection states is recorded as PIA's state. The line buys no analyst request.
- `TV_IF` may not be `wgpia0` (it already could not be `tun0`), and `WAN_IF` may be neither: the input chain drops new connections from both names whatever `VPN_IF` says. `install.sh`, `tvgw-apply` and `config.tv_side` refuse them alike.
- The .deb's postrm sets `nf_conntrack_events` back to the kernel's default, 2, as it removes the sysctl file, so a later conntrack listener hears events without a reboot.
- Design decisions 1 and 9 say why the ISP does not read the TV's DNS (DoH by address carries no server name; the tunnel hides the home address from the DoH provider; the ISP reads the TV's own server names on the direct path anyway), and what AdGuard's optimistic cache does and does not cover. ### Capture v2: dumpcap, and a first-packets prototype behind CAPTURE_MODE (2026-11-01)

- `tvgw-pcap.service` runs dumpcap in place of tcpdump (design decision 38, [the capture page](docs/how-it-works/capture.md)): pcap format (`-P`), group-readable files (`-g`; dumpcap's own default is `0600`, unreadable to the extractor), a new file on every exact hour of the clock (`-b interval:3600`) whether or not a packet arrives, named `tv_<UTC time>_<n>.pcap` with the time first (`-b nametimenum:2`). The filter is unchanged. tcpdump's `-G` rotated an hour after its own start and only on a packet (data-architecture finding 6, state-of-the-art section 1.2). dumpcap comes with `wireshark-common`, a dependency of tshark; a missing or non-executable dumpcap fails the start with a message saying what to install. The deploy restarts the capture once.
- `pcaps.live_files()` treats `dumpcap` and `tcpdump` (`pcaps.WRITERS`) as the writers whose open files are live; the ingest reads a dumpcap ring exactly as the tcpdump ring. The ingest and retention warnings say "the capture" instead of "tcpdump".
- New setting `CAPTURE_MODE=full|headers`, default `full` (design decision 39). With `headers`, `tvgw-apply` loads its own table `inet tvgw_capture`, which hands the first 8 packets of each TV connection (4096 bytes each) and every later packet to TCP port 80 to NFLOG group 7, and `tvgw-pcap-headers.service` writes them with dumpcap as `tvhdr_<UTC time>_<n>.pcap`. The table only counts and logs, and a failure to load it only warns. A prototype: see [measuring it on the Pi](docs/how-it-works/capture.md#measuring-it-on-the-pi).
- `tvgw verify`: `services` expects the capture unit the mode names, a new `capture` check fails when both capture units run or, in headers mode, when the capture rule is missing, `applied_current` covers `CAPTURE_MODE`, and the capture table's counters are recorded in headers mode.
- The capture table's two rules send each packet to NFLOG at once (`queue-threshold 1`): batched, a SYN or a hello could carry a time up to a second late. [The measurements](docs/how-it-works/capture.md#measuring-it-on-the-pi) gain a fifth step that compares the frame times with the kernel's.
- The brief's JSON carries the capture table's counters under `capture_counters`, not in `firewall.activity`, so neither the brief nor the analyst's pack presents the packets handed to NFLOG as something the firewall held.
- The hardware suite's capture liveness check needs a recent pcap that holds a packet: dumpcap's hourly files exist whether or not anything is captured.

### Extractor: JA4, split and nameless hellos, QUIC Initial names, schema 8 (2026-11-01)

R&D state of the art 2026-10, sections 1.1, 1.2, 10 and 11; [the extractor](docs/how-it-works/extractor.md); design decisions 33 to 37.

- tshark's one pass keeps every ClientHello, with or without a server name, and the held-back first segment of a hello split over TCP segments the capture did not all keep. `clienthello.py` (new) reads such a segment, recovering the server name when it lies there; that hello is `partial` and has no JA4. Reassembly preferences are stated on the command line.
- JA4 is computed for every hello read whole, from tshark's fields (the same ones in tshark 4.0 and 4.4; the native `tls.handshake.ja4` of 4.2+ is never asked for) or from the bytes. The `signature_algorithms` list is told apart from the two other lists tshark prints under the same field; when they do not add up no fingerprint is stored.
- A hello inside a QUIC Initial is marked `transport = 'quic'` (tshark decrypts the Initial itself); its JA4 begins with `q`.
- Repeats are dropped: one hello per transport and four-tuple within 30 seconds, a whole hello replacing a partial one (`tshark.dedupe`). `extract(keep_repeats=True)` keeps them for comparisons; `tshark.summary` counts a file's hellos by what could be read.
- The tshark sandbox gains private IPC, protected kernel logs, clock, hostname and process list, no namespaces, realtime or set-id files, a locked personality, a private keyring, `umask 077`, no core dumps and `TasksMax=64` (`tshark.HARDENING`). A refused system call still kills tshark. Root without `systemd-run` is still refused.
- Store schema 8, migrated in place: `tls.ja4`, `tls.transport`, `tls.partial`, the `tls_ja4` index (created after the columns, `db.LATE_INDEXES`), and `tls_noname` for hellos without a name, pruned with the other facts. `pcaps_done.tls_rows` still counts named hellos. `tvgw size` lists the new table.
- `queries.tls_noname` lists nameless hellos by address, port, transport and JA4, with the names the same fingerprint carried in the window and the week before; the window report and `tvgw brief --json` carry it as `tls_noname`.
- The ECH canary counts a hello under a known ECH public name (`queries.ECH_PUBLIC_NAMES`: `cloudflare-ech.com`) as real ECH even when the TV looked the name up.
- The ingest's extraction-fault warning (at least 20 connection attempts to :443 in a file) now asks for any hello of the TV's, named or not, so a file whose hellos all lack a name no longer warns that none was found.
- Hardware suite: `test_the_sandboxed_tshark_reads_the_newest_finished_pcap` (new) runs the real sandbox on the newest finished pcap at every deploy, so a hardening property the Pi refuses fails CI instead of only warning in the hourly ingest. `test_finished_pcaps_yield_rows_while_the_tv_is_on` fails, instead of skipping, when every pcap read in the last 4 hours carries an error.
- `tests/corpora/` (new): synthesised ClientHello pcaps (the JA4 spec's worked example, a post-quantum split hello whole and cut, three signature lists, no SNI, real ECH, TLS 1.2 with an odd ALPN sent twice, an encrypted QUIC v1 Initial), their tshark rows and the expected hellos, with `build.py` and `record.py`. `test_corpora.py` runs them through the rows, the bytes and, where installed, tshark; `test_clienthello.py` holds JA4's properties over random hellos.

### Setup: `tvgw setup`, the guided installer (2026-11-01)

- `tvgw setup` (src/tvgw/setup.py) finds the TV with `tvgw discover`'s sources, asks which device it is, asks for `TV_NAME`, `WAN_IF` and `WAN_GW` with defaults from the settings file and the Pi's default route, offers to pair an LG TV's LAN API read-only, shows the settings-file diff, writes it and runs `install.sh` under the install lock, then runs `tvgw verify`'s checks and says in plain words whether the gateway holds (design decision 28; R&D §9). Pairing says what to accept on the TV and that the TV's agreements stay declined; it waits for the install when the LAN-API library is not there yet.
- `--answers FILE --yes` installs unattended. The answers file is the settings file's format plus `SETUP_TV`, `SETUP_PAIR`, `SETUP_APPLY`, `SETUP_SSDP`, `SETUP_WAIT` and `SETUP_INSTALLER`, checked whole before the first step; an unattended run stops rather than guess the TV or the router (design decision 30). Re-running is safe: nothing new means nothing written and no install.

They refuse an unknown key, any value with shell syntax in it, a malformed `TV_MAC`, `TV_NAME` or address, and any file `config.load()` or `bash -n` would refuse; a line of a changed key that is not a plain `KEY=VALUE` line is refused, not rewritten (design decision 29).

- `install.sh` restarts `tvgw-pcap`, `tvgw-flowlog` and `tvgw-dnsgate` when what each read at its last start changed (its unit file, `TV_IP` for the flow log, the settings and the package for the feeder), from a hash kept in `/var/lib/tvgw/.unit-stamps` and written after the restart; comparing unit files alone left the flow log on the old `TV_IP` and the feeder on the old address and the old code until a reboot.
- The same record makes a restart that an install did not reach happen on the next run: the restart list used to be decided before the units were copied and lost when the install stopped on the way to the restart (a failed `tvgw adguard apply`, a rejected schedule), after which the files compared equal and the old capture definition ran until a reboot.
- The install lock covers the rsync that replaces `/opt/tvgw/src` and the CI hardware suite: both deploy paths go through `deploy/sync-install.sh`, which takes the lock, rsyncs, runs `install.sh` with the lock handed down on its descriptor, then the suite (`make deploy` stages the tree in `~/tvgw-deploy/` on the Pi first, and `make hardware` takes the lock too); before, either path's `rsync --delete` could replace files under the other's running install, and a `make deploy` during the CI suite left the green check certifying a tree that was not what was tested.
- `tvgw discover` receives SSDP answers with the firewall loaded: the search socket is bound before it sends, and for the search only the exemption also accepts UDP from the TV side to that socket's port in the input chain, where the TV's unicast answer (a new flow to conntrack) met `TV may not reach other Pi services`; both holes are closed again afterwards.
- `install.sh` removes the checkout's `build/` before `pip install` (setuptools never prunes `build/lib` and the rsync exclude list protects it from `--delete`, so `report.py`, retired from `src/`, kept shipping in every wheel), and `deploy/rsync.exclude` lists everything `.gitignore` does, so `make deploy` no longer copies a virtualenv, pcaps, logs, `deploy/local/`, `.pi` or `backups/` to the Pi.
- What survives a power cut: `install.sh` runs `sync` once everything the next boot reads is written (a cut soon after a deploy could leave `tvgw-apply` or a unit empty and the TV dark), `tvgw discover --write` replaces the settings file whole through an fsynced file beside it with the old mode and owner instead of rewriting it in place, and `tvgw backup` fsyncs the copy before naming it and the directory after (`VACUUM INTO` does not), and removes a temp copy over an hour old that a run killed at its timeout left behind.

### Firewall and deploy: the gateway as a system (2026-10-27)

- `tvgw verify` sees two states that were invisible: `applied_current` fails when `tvgw.conf`, `block4.txt` or `doh4.txt` is newer than the last successful apply by more than two minutes (a `block4.txt` line nft rejects leaves the old set loaded and `ruleset.applied` unwritten, so `ruleset_unchanged` kept passing while every re-apply failed only in the journal and the next boot would have left the TV dark), and `doh4_fresh` fails when the DoH list is over eight days old or missing. Both read the host through a new `mtime` seam. The key-rule needles are `verify.KEY_RULES`, which the deploy lint checks against `tvgw-apply`'s text. The forward chain's catch-all is labelled `other forward drops` in the brief.
- Settings are exact on both sides: `tvgw-apply` defaults `UPLINK`, `WAN_IF`, `BLOCK_QUIC` and `STRICT_PORTS` as `config.Settings` does (a conf written before a switch existed aborted every apply under `set -u`, the one at boot included), refuses a switch that is not exactly `yes` or `no` (`BLOCK_QUIC=Yes` dropped the QUIC reject silently and `verify`, reading the same value, stopped looking for it) and requires `WAN_GW` up front in direct mode; `config.load` refuses the same near misses, and a conf that exists but cannot be read is an error, not the defaults.
- `install.sh` refuses unusable identity settings before writing anything (`${TV_MAC:?}` let the template's all-zero MAC through to dnsmasq; a `TV_NAME` with `/` or `&` broke or rewrote the sed expression), tests the nginx config and stops on a rejection instead of skipping it inside an `&&` list, reloads nginx rather than restarting it (the TLS passthrough and downloads in progress survive), fails on an enable failure instead of `|| true`, takes a lock so `make deploy` and a CI deploy cannot interleave, and ends with `exec tvgw verify`, so a FAIL fails `make deploy` and the CI deploy step instead of being echoed away. It installs `doh-exclude.txt` unconditionally, as every document already said; fetches the DoH list on a gateway that has none instead of waiting up to a day for the timer; brings `tv-lan` up only when it is down (re-activating it dropped `10.10.10.1` for a moment and fired a dispatcher apply that reset the counters); and makes `/etc/tvgw/tvgw.conf` `640 root:adm`, the one file holding the TV's MAC and the router's address and the only one the permission test did not cover.
- `tvgw-doh-update` stages the new list beside its destination and renames it over the old one (GNU `install` unlinked and rewrote the file, and the `mktemp` in `/tmp` was on another filesystem, so a dispatcher apply in that window loaded an empty set); it fetches over HTTPS only, follows redirects only to HTTPS, caps the body at 8 MB, and refuses to run without `doh-exclude.txt`, which would have blocked every CDN edge in the list.
- Every `Type=oneshot` unit has a `TimeoutStartSec`: a hung `tvgw-apply` at boot held `multi-user.target` and dnsmasq for ever, and a hung verify ended the 10-minute snapshots, since `OnUnitActiveSec` re-arms only when the unit finishes. A timed-out apply leaves the TV unforwarded, the fail-closed state.
- CI: the workflow-level `concurrency` that could cancel `sudo ./install.sh` on the production Pi mid-run is gone; unit jobs cancel per ref and Python version, the hardware job queues behind a running deploy and has 90 minutes (a first analyze run can take 45 and the suite runs ingest twice); a push that changes only Markdown runs nothing (a docs-only deploy bounced nginx, dnsmasq, the flow log and `tv-lan` and reset every counter); both deploy paths use `deploy/rsync.exclude`; root gets one environment variable, not the runner's whole environment. The NetworkManager dispatcher reads `WAN_IF` from the conf instead of hardcoding `wlan0`.
- Hardware suite: every file `install.sh` copies is byte-equal to the checkout and the generated dnsmasq and nginx files equal their templates (nothing had linked the installed tree to the repository); every unit and timer is `enabled`; `nftables.service` is masked; the route test demands the device the settings name (`wan_if or tun0` passed with the TV's packets going into the tunnel in direct mode, which `tv_out` drops); idempotency is judged by natural key against the rows present before the second run instead of raw counts, which failed whenever the gateway was in use; the DNS-chain test reads the store's counter snapshots instead of live counters the install zeroes seconds earlier, so it no longer skips on every CI run; the settings file's mode, a leftover staged DoH list and the list's age are checked.
- `tests/unit/test_deploy_lint.py`: shell syntax for every script as its shebang says, the key-rule text `verify` and the hardware suite look for present in `tvgw-apply`, the files the hardware suite compares present in the checkout, a start timeout on every oneshot, the workflow's shape, the one rsync list, and `tvgw-apply`'s defaults and `case` arms equal to `Settings` and `config.CHOICES`.

### Policy: profiles, AdGuard as code, the URL filter (2026-10-27)

- The generated must-allow exceptions carry `$important`. Order never decided AdGuard's precedence: a `$important` block in any list beat the plain `@@||host^` exception the clock host depended on, and only an exception with the modifier wins over both. The four `$dnsrewrite` rules for the GFTS hosts are generated from `[url_filter].hosts` and `GW_IP` instead of hardcoding `10.10.10.1` in the `.rules` file (byte-identical with the default settings), and a unit test fails `make gate` when either nginx file's regex, `server_name` list or passthrough map disagrees with the profile. The live answers do not change; the rule text does, so the next deploy is one validated apply.
- A profile is read whole or not at all. A TOML loaded without its `.rules` file was nine exceptions and no block, with a clean lint, and `apply` would have replaced AdGuard's rules with that list; a renamed `[profile].name` or an unreadable sibling gave zero custom rules the same way. `load()` now reads the companion by the name asked for and fails on a missing or unreadable file (an empty one is the capture-phase profile). Mistyped TOML fails at load naming the key: a bare string where a list was meant (eleven one-character patterns), a misspelt table, an unknown expected category, a category named `Other`, a `block_regex` that does not compile, a filter without `name` and `url`; strip prefixes are lower-cased. The CLI turns an unloadable profile into one line and exit 1.
- The lint sees what it missed: hosts-syntax blocks (`0.0.0.0 host`), `$dnsrewrite` rules and regex rules covering a must-allow host, a regex that does not compile, a wildcard-only chain above a protected apex (`*.lgtvsdp.com`), an exception equal to a block (the block is dead), and a URL-filter host above a must-allow host. `tvgw adguard diff` and `apply` refuse a profile that does not lint, so a profile loaded from a path is never applied unlinted.
- `plan()` owns every key that can unfilter the TV: `blocking_mode` (`default`, the `0.0.0.0` answers `verify`, `dnsmsg` and the hardware suite read as blocked), `protection_disabled_until`, `upstream_dns_file`, and the TV client's own filtering, global-settings and upstream fields. Each of these had survived every deploy when edited in the UI, because `changes()` reports only what `plan()` sets. Client reconciliation compares ids case-insensitively and cannot leave two clients carrying the TV's MAC, which AdGuard refuses to start on; a filter with `id: null` and a repeated list URL are handled; upstreams are trimmed and an empty `DNS_UPSTREAMS` is refused.
- `apply()` is a guarded transaction: one at a time (a lock beside the file), an empty or truncated config refused before anything is touched, the candidate validated with AdGuard's own rejection text, the live file copied to `AdGuardHome.yaml.bak-<UTC stamp>Z-<pid>` before the stop (never over an earlier backup), SIGTERM/SIGHUP/SIGINT held off while the service is down, and every failure from the stop onwards puts the backup back by the same atomic write and restarts the unit. Before, a failing `start` left the planned file live with the backup unrestored, a failing `stop` leaked the candidate, and the rollback copied in place, so a kill mid-copy truncated the live YAML. Readiness means the first must-allow name resolves (NOERROR or NXDOMAIN): a resolver answering SERVFAIL used to pass, leaving the TV without names.
- YAML 1.2 both ways: an RFC 3339 scalar (which `protection_disabled_until` holds while protection is paused) stays text and is written back plain; `1e3` and `0o17` are quoted on output, so a 1.2 reader cannot take them for numbers.
- The DNS decoder rejects reserved label types, names over 255 octets, rdata overruns and truncated questions, and writes label bytes outside letters, digits, `-` and `_` as `\DDD`, so a CNAME label can no longer forge an `A:0.0.0.0` answer inside the stored text. `dnsmsg.rcode()` reads the response code.
- The categorizer runs in O(labels): plain patterns are one dictionary lookup per label suffix and wildcard patterns are compiled once, with results unchanged by construction (0.98 s to 0.043 s per 20 000 names; `refresh_hosts` recategorizes every name whenever the rules change).
- The nginx URL-filter log carries `$request_method $request_length "$upstream_status"`, so the filter's own 403 is told from one LG's host returned, and gfts rows carry the method once a line has it (NULL before, nothing invented). A unit test ties the parser to the deployed `log_format`. Doc corrections: `tools.google.com` is `3rd-party ads`, not `Infra`; the nginx header says `URL_FILTER_RESOLVER`.

### Analysis: the brief, the queries, the verify checks (2026-10-27)

- The brief alarms on what used to be silent. `verify` is a Coverage source, `NOT ASSESSED` when the watchdog did not run in the two hours before the window closed; a red line when fewer than half the 10-minute runs the window owed were recorded (counted from the first run the store ever saw, so a fresh store is not alarmed); a red line when the TV was on for an hour with no lookups or no connections recorded, naming the producer; a red line while the TV's Live Plus (ACR) setting is not off, with a new TV settings section listing the readings. The clock rule is judged over whole days only. Red lines come in the order to act on them, and every report records when it was generated.
- "Firewall held" counts right: rules sharing a comment (the UDP and TCP DNS redirects) are one series instead of a reset at every snapshot (296 where 61 happened), snapshots are compared in the order taken so a clock stepped back after a power loss is not a reset, and the baseline is two index seeks rather than a lookup per rule. `FW_LINES` covers every counted drop and reject in `tvgw-apply` (the forward catch-all, the wrong-uplink drop, the LG 8080 fallback, the IPv6 wire drop, which `verify --record` now snapshots too); the dead "unexpected port" prefix is gone and `tests/unit/test_fw_lines.py` reads the script to keep the two in step.
- Standby traffic is attributed by when a connection was opened, not by conntrack's destroy time two hours later, and two readings further than 90 minutes apart are a missed reading, not an off hour. Unresolved flows are explained only by an A answer that names the address exactly. Reverse-lookup sweeps cover the firewall's whole `lan4` set (172.18 and 172.19 were missed, 172.32-39 counted) through one address classification in `names.py` that does not change with the Python version, which the LAN-probe test in ingest now uses too (`names.is_local_address`: a SYN to a CGNAT address, which the firewall drops, is a probe; one to a documentation range is not); a punycode TLD is a name.
- New section: blocked names still reached over TLS, the join that proves a filter bypass. `dns_anomalies` judges fan-out per day, reports real lookup counts and flags all-hex identifiers. The firmware section starts its look-ahead an hour before the reading. The TV line collapses readings into power runs and is dated in reports longer than a day.
- `tvgw verify`: an empty answer for a must-allow name fails (AdGuard's nxdomain and refused modes, an upstream outage); a loaded table without its apply baseline fails; `tvgw.service` and the four timers are watched; the DoH set is measured in addresses, not merged elements. The verdict is printed before the store is touched, and a run that cannot be recorded is one stderr line and exit 1 instead of a traceback or silence.
- `tvgw host` reads flows through a `flows(dst, sport, t_end)` index (166 s to 0.05 s on a 100-day synthetic store) and credits a flow to the one name the brief gives it; `flows_dst` is dropped and `flows(t_start)` added. Every ranked query has a deterministic tie order. `clean()` escapes every non-printable code point, bidi overrides included. Malformed CLI time arguments are usage errors (exit 2), the report's window flags are exclusive, and `--since` must precede `--until`.
- Tests for the report path (week bounds, the weekly table, `tvgw report` through the CLI, plain HTTP, DNS bypass), the `FW_LINES` contract, the address classification, and query plans read back through SQLite's trace callback.

### Ingest and store: organizing the data (2026-10-27)

- The TV power history is tab-separated. webOS states contain spaces (`Active Standby`, `Screen Off`) and were split into power `Active` with an app of `Standby ...`; `unknown` now means the TV answered but the power read failed, `unreachable` that it did not answer. The old lines still read.
- Schema version 3: time indexes on `oddport` and `fw_counters`, `tls_dst` dropped (a prefix of `tls_conn`), the one index drop that lived in the schema moved into `_migrate`, and a new `ingest_runs` table with one row per run. A report's Coverage lists the warnings of the runs that fed its window instead of the last run's. Writers run `synchronous=FULL`.
- Tail sources commit every 10 000 lines together with their cursor, so a backlog neither fills memory nor holds the write lock for the whole run, and a run that dies loses only the batch in flight. The cheap sources are read first; tshark has a 20-minute budget per run and does not reassemble HTTP bodies; the analyze unit has `OOMScoreAdjust=500`.
- A source whose input is missing is a warning and `NOT ASSESSED` (the TV-state history excepted). `journalctl` failures surface instead of reading as a quiet port log, and the journal is read with `_TRANSPORT=kernel` rather than `-k`, which hid every line written before a reboot. An unreadable policy file warns. Lines the flow and URL-filter logs cannot parse are counted.
- The query-log parser is total and typed: a line that is not a record raises and is counted, oddly typed fields are coerced, so one record can no longer roll back the hour and every hour after it. `user_rules_from_yaml` returns None, not `[]`, for a file without a `user_rules` list, so it can no longer retire every rule in the history.
- The hosts watermark records its row's time and rebuilds when a table emptied and SQLite reused the ids; categories are recomputed only when the profile's rules change. `fw_counters` and `verify` are pruned under `DB_DAYS`, and the "Firewall held" line's previous-snapshot lookup is an index range per rule. `VACUUM` records its attempt first and runs only when a quarter of the file is free.
- Store only what the source says: LAN-API read errors are not settings, URL-filter rows carry no invented `GET`. Settings: integer fields parse by the default's type and `TV_IP` and `GW_IP` must be addresses. Names longer than DNS allows are not public names; pcap names and hosts are cut at 2000 characters.
- `tvgw-analyze` writes finished days and weeks once their evidence has settled (`SETTLE`, three hours), one rule for both loops. New tests pin the mutants that survived (the rollback in `step()`, the malformed count, the tvstate replace, `last_seen`, the store's file mode) and the DST reason for UTC pcap names.

### Capture: what leaves the TV and how we see it (2026-10-27)

- The capture keeps only frames the TV sends: SYN/FIN/RST, requests to port 80, UDP except QUIC short headers, a TCP segment only when it begins a TLS ClientHello record, and a segment that begins with an HTTP method (LG's :8080 firmware fallback). The old filter kept any segment whose first byte was 0x16, about one ciphertext segment in 256, and both directions of port 80 including response bodies. `tests/unit/test_capture_filter.py` runs the deployed expression through tcpdump on a synthetic hour.
- Pcap names are UTC with seconds (`tv-YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ.pcap`): local-time names repeat in the DST fall-back hour and tcpdump truncates a file whose name it has used before. `install.sh` restarts a capture unit only when its file changed; it never restarted `tvgw-pcap` at all.
- Which pcap tcpdump is writing is read from its open descriptors in `/proc` (`pcaps.py`), not from name order; when that cannot be told, ingest skips the pcaps and warns rather than read a half-written hour.
- `pcaps_done` records per file the error and the number of attempts: a file tshark cannot read is retried hourly for a day, then recorded as unreadable; one warning per run covers every failing file; a file too short to be a capture is recorded without running tshark. `syn443_rows` keeps the TV's connection attempts to :443, and a file with twenty or more of them and no hello raises a warning (the 2026-10-27 fault looked like twelve quiet hours).
- A connection attempt is counted once however often its SYN is retransmitted; a request to the gateway is stored once, from nginx's log, not again from the pcap.
- Pcap retention moved into the package: `tvgw prune-pcaps` (hourly) deletes only files ingest has read, never the live file, keeps and reports an un-read file until twice `PCAP_DAYS`, holds a 1 GB free-space floor, and exits 1 on any warning. `deploy/bin/tvgw-prune` is retired; `PCAP_DAYS` and `PCAP_MAX_GB` are validated settings.
- `tvgw-flowlog` honours `TV_IP` from the conf (systemd expands `${VAR}` in `ExecStart`; `$$` is the escape) and creates `flows.log` 640.
- conntrack lines that cannot be stored (NaN times, a counter past 64 bits, a nonsense duration) no longer roll the flows step back every hour; the module says what `t_start` and `t_end` are. tshark rows are framed on newline alone, must have exactly the documented fields, cap names at 255, and root without `setpriv` is refused. Log tailing reads identity, fingerprint and lines from one descriptor and warns when a rotation lost lines.
- Finished days and weeks are written from 03:20, once idle connections and the last pcap have landed.

### Autonomous blocking (2026-10-28, design decision 20)

- `ANALYST_APPLY=auto`: a `block` the daily analyst proposes is applied by the gateway itself when the code's gates pass (vetted, low risk, the name looked up on two or more days, a traffic shape with no payload: median under 16 KB down per connection and under 2 MB in the window, from the flows the TLS hello named; at most three a day), a second, different model (`ANALYST_VERIFIER_MODEL`, `claude-fable-5-1` at xhigh by default; `verifier_for` never returns the proposer) fails to refute it, and AdGuard takes it. The rule lives in a machine-owned file (`AUTO_RULES`, `/var/lib/tvgw/auto.rules`) that `tvgw adguard apply` appends to the profile's rules after the same lint; the profile's `.rules` is never edited by the machine.
- Three watches after a block: a live canary (bytes to the TV and the LG foreground app for ten minutes; three readings under a fifth of the pre-block rate while streaming, or the app giving way to the launcher, revert within minutes), an hourly judge (`tvgw-autoblock.timer`, new: streaming volume per TV hour against the three days before, the must-allow and clock red lines, the trial closing after a day) and the owner's `tvgw undo` (`--all`); deleting the file reverts everything at the next check, and the record follows. Every machine rule, verdict and reason is in the store (`auto_rules`, schema 6), on the proposal (`Applied:`), in `tvgw autoblock list` and in the brief's new "Rules the gateway added on its own" section.
- Token economy: `novelty()` decides whether a window deserves a request at all. The TV off with no lookups, or only names judged within the last week (`judgments`: every proposal remembered by name, except a block the gates refused for too few days, the daily cap or a transient failure, which is asked again) with no red line, blocked name reached, ACR-like cadence, gate sample, unresolved destination or firmware change, writes a one-line file and sends nothing; `--force` asks anyway. The pack carries each candidate's traffic shape (`shapes`) and the previous judgements, so the model reasons from how the TV used a name rather than from the name alone.
- Found while testing: `db.connect` left its schema-version `INSERT` in an open transaction, so the connection held the write lock until its first commit and every other writer waited (`database is locked` after the analyst's 30 s timeout whenever another writer-capable connection was open); it commits now. The analyst and weekly units may write AdGuard's config (`ReadWritePaths` gains `/opt/AdGuardHome`); the hourly check runs in its own unit rather than inside the sandboxed `tvgw-analyze`, whose read-only system would have made a revert's re-apply fail silently; a re-apply that fails is written on the decision and fails the unit.

### The daily analyst (2026-10-28, design decision 19)

- `tvgw analyst` and `tvgw-analyst.timer` (03:50): yesterday's evidence pack, minimized (names, counts, categories, rules, public server addresses, hours on, apps seen; never the TV's or LAN's addresses, MACs, device or host names, probe targets, reverse-lookup names, request paths or an hourly timeline, with a scrub over every string and a hardware test on the real pack), goes to `claude-opus-5-5` in one streamed request answered inside a JSON schema. Proposals (block, allow, unblock, watch, question) are vetted like profile rules and written to `reports/proposals/<day>.md` and `.json` with tokens and cost. Nothing is applied. The key is `/etc/tvgw/claude.key`, root-only; without it the job skips; `--dry-run` writes the pack and prompt and sends nothing; `--show` prints the newest proposals. New optional dependency `anthropic` (`pip install tvgw[analyst]`, installed by `install.sh`). `ANALYST_MODEL` and `ANALYST_EFFORT` in `tvgw.conf` choose the model and the reasoning depth; the day's record keeps both and the cost at the model's list price. `--model`, `--effort`, `--since Nh` and `--day` set one run; `ANALYST_SCHEDULE` (time of day, `hourly`, or a systemd calendar expression, rendered into a timer drop-in by `install.sh`) and `ANALYST_WINDOW` (`day` or `Nh`) set the cadence and the window; a comparison run with another model writes `<stem>.<model>.md`. A weekly pass (`tvgw-analyst-weekly.timer`, Monday 04:10) reads the whole ISO week that just ended with its own model and effort (`ANALYST_WEEKLY_MODEL`, default `claude-fable-5-1`; `off` disables), the owner's choice after a side-by-side run of both models on one day: the same proposal, a richer narrative from Fable at three times the price, so Opus 5.5 reads the days and Fable the weeks.

### DNS-gated egress, count-only (2026-10-28, design decision 18)

- `DNSGATE=count` (default): `tvgw-dnsgate` streams AdGuard's answers to the TV through tcpdump and adds every IPv4 address to `inet tvgw_gate resolved4` with a one-day timeout; `tvgw-apply` keeps that table (its own table, `add` statements, so re-applies keep the set and `verify`'s baseline stays static) with one rule that counts, and logs six samples a minute of, each new TV connection to an address the set does not hold. Nothing is dropped; `DNSGATE=off` removes it all.
- The brief has a DNS-gated egress section: the count ("to addresses DNS never gave" in Firewall held) and the samples with the TLS name a hello gave each address, so a forgotten lookup reads differently from a hardcoded server. The samples are a new store table (`gate`, schema 5) fed from the kernel log like the unexpected-port lines; `verify` checks the table, the set and the feeder, and reports the set's size.
- The feeder lets tcpdump's own stderr reach the journal (a feeder that dies at once left only `tcpdump ended (rc=1)` behind, 167 times in a quarter hour between two deploys on 2026-10-28, when the pre-fix build tried to drop privileges), and its restarts back off from 5 s to 5 min (`RestartSteps`, `RestartMaxDelaySec`) instead of every five seconds for ever.
- Settings, `tvgw-apply` and `config.load` agree on the new switch (the deploy lint checks it), and the feeder runs as root bounded to `CAP_NET_RAW` and `CAP_NET_ADMIN` with a read-only system; its tcpdump keeps root (`-Z root`), since Debian's default drop to the `tcpdump` user needs a capability the unit does not grant.

### Analysis v2 (2026-10-27, from the R&D survey)

- The brief has a Periodic connections section: names (bare addresses when no hello named them) the TV connects to on a timer, found by the median and median absolute deviation of start-to-start intervals (MAD/median under 0.15, period 10 s to a day, at least 12 events; connections opened within 5 s are one event, so a page load is not a beacon and a Poisson stream never passes). Each row carries the median payload bound per connection. On the first two days of data it names a 10-minute heartbeat to an Akamai address that no hello or lookup explains.
- ACR cadence canary: a periodic name with a 10 to 20 s period and under 4 KB per connection is marked `ACR-like`; under a category that is not the TV doing its job it is a red line (LG's ACR client uploads about every 15 s, Anselmi et al., IMC 2024).
- Top upload is honest about bytes: a new `payload<=` column strips 40 B of IP and TCP headers per packet from conntrack's count, so TCP acknowledgements of a download no longer read as upload.

### Capture (2026-10-27, from the R&D survey)

- tcpdump never runs as root: `tvgw-pcap.service` starts as the system user `tvgw-pcap` with `CAP_NET_RAW` and `CAP_NET_ADMIN` as its whole capability set, a read-only system and only the pcap directory writable (before, it started as root and dropped to the user with `-Z`). Files, names and the filter are unchanged; a hardware test reads the running process's uid and bounding set.
- dumpcap was tried and not adopted: it creates every file `0600`, which the extractor (running as `nobody`) cannot read, and it renames files to its own `prefix_NNNNN_time` scheme. Hour-aligned rotation waits for the position-based capture prototype.

### Storage resilience (2026-10-27, from the R&D survey)

- `tvgw backup` and `tvgw-backup.timer` (daily 03:40): `VACUUM INTO` a fresh copy of the store in `/var/lib/tvgw/backup/`, `PRAGMA quick_check` before the copy gets its UTC name, the newest seven kept, `640 root:adm`. `install.sh` runs the first one, `verify` counts the timer among the services, the hardware suite fails on a stale or unsound copy, and `make backup` pulls the copies to your machine. Nothing took a backup before.
- The hardware watchdog needed no work: trixie's systemd runs with `RuntimeWatchdogSec` set (the BCM2835 driver caps the timeout at 15 s), so a hung kernel reboots on its own, fail closed.

### DNS hardening and supply chain (2026-10-27, from the R&D survey)

- The TV gets no HTTPS/SVCB answers: one generated AdGuard rule, scoped to those record types and to the TV's address (`[adguard] strip_qtypes` in the profile). Those records carry Encrypted ClientHello keys, which would hide every server name from the capture, address hints that skip the A lookup the gateway watches, and HTTP/3 hints the TV cannot use. Clients fall back to A records (RFC 9460); the CX has never asked for either type. The lint knows `$dnstype` rules are typed, not blocks.
- `adguard apply` owns `dns.aaaa_disabled` (true: the TV segment is v4-only with IPv6 dropped at the wire, so AAAA answers are withheld locally instead of fetched, which halves the upstream queries) and `dns.handle_ddr`.
- `tvgw verify` has an `adguard_version` check: AdGuard Home must be at least v0.107.73, the release that fixed an authentication bypass over h2c. The version is recorded in the store and shown in the brief's Coverage line.
- ECH canary: every TLS hello row records whether it carried the encrypted_client_hello extension (`tls.ech`, schema 4); the first such handshake is a red line, with the outer names, because per-connection naming starts going dark that day.
- tshark runs inside a transient systemd unit (`systemd-run --wait --pipe`) as `nobody:nogroup` with no network, a read-only system, no devices, no new privileges or capabilities, the service syscall set, a memory limit and a time limit; before, `setpriv` dropped the user and nothing else. Exit codes and output pass through unchanged, so the "cut short" reading of exit 2 still holds.
- CI: the GitHub Actions are pinned by commit with the version in a comment, and dependabot proposes the bumps; a deploy lint fails on an unpinned action.

### Platform: Debian 13 (2026-10-27)

- The reference gateway runs Debian 13 (trixie): kernel 6.18, Python 3.13, tshark 4.4.19, nftables 1.1.3, NetworkManager 1.52. Nothing in tvgw changed for it; after the virtualenv was rebuilt the hardware suite passed unchanged. The in-place upgrade procedure and its two snags (needrestart restarting the runner, Pi desktop file conflicts) are in [operations.md](docs/use/daily-brief.md). Python 3.11 stays the floor and CI tests both interpreters.

### Discovery: the installer finds the TV

- `tvgw discover [--write]` lists the devices on the TV side from the DHCP lease (webOS announces itself as `LGwebOSTV`), the neighbour table, the names in dnsmasq's log, the vendor LAN-API ports a TV listens on (webOS 3000/3001, Tizen 8001/8002, Roku 8060, SmartCast 7345/9000, Cast 8008/8009) and an SSDP search when the TV still answers one; `--write` records `TV_MAC`, `TV_IP`, `TV_NAME` and the matching `PROFILE`. `install.sh` brings the TV side up first and runs it when the settings still hold the placeholder MAC, so nobody has to look up a MAC address. The SSDP `LOCATION` is fetched only when it is plain http to the address that answered, with redirects refused and the body capped at 64 KiB: the installer runs as root and a device on the TV segment must not be able to point it at `file://` or at the gateway's own loopback services (found by the October 2026 R&D survey). `tvgw tvcheck` records the retail model (`getSystemInfo` `modelName`, e.g. `OLED55CXPUA`), LG's platform code and the country code as separate readings; `tvgw profile` without `--model` describes the model the TV reported.

### Fixes after the first day in production

- Profile note: LAN-API writes on webOS 5 firmware 04.64 are confirmed firmware-enforced read-only (the Luna bridge answers `isCallAllowed ... false` with the full manifest), so the TV's settings and saved Wi-Fi networks cannot be changed from the gateway.

- The unprivileged tshark could not open the pcaps once the state directory was closed (2026-10-27): tshark exits 2 for that as well as for "cut short", so twelve pcaps were marked done with zero rows. Exit 2 now counts as "cut short" only when tshark says so; pcaps are 640 root:nogroup; the hardware suite checks that finished pcaps yield rows while the TV is on.
- Log tailing keeps a fingerprint of each file's first bytes, so a copytruncate that regrew past the old offset is recognized instead of being read mid-line.
- TV readings are stamped in ISO form with the UTC offset, so November's repeated 01:20 cannot collapse two readings; the old form is still read.

### Reports from the store (legacy pipeline retired)

- `tvgw report` renders the brief over any window: `--day`, `--week YYYY-Www`, `--since/--until`, or the last 24 h. It adds the sections the legacy report still had over the brief: a by-category table (lookups, blocked, names, connections, bytes), plain-HTTP request groups, DNS the TV sent to other resolvers, and a by-day table for weeks.
- `tvgw-analyze` writes `reports/latest`, `reports/daily/<day>` and `reports/weekly/<week>` (text and JSON) from the store. `report.py`, `tvgw-extract`, the TSV archive and the `ARCHIVE_DAYS`/`REPORT_WINDOW` settings are gone; `install.sh` removes the derived files and moves pre-store reports to `reports/legacy/`. One tshark pass per pcap instead of two.

### Firewall and routing

- `tvgw-apply` is fail-closed. One apply runs at a time (lock). The ruleset loads atomically and forwarding is enabled last, so a bad rule file never leaves the TV forwarded without a filter. Stopping `tvgw.service` turns forwarding off on the TV interface first: the TV goes dark, not unfiltered.
- Fixed: after a restart the TV had no route, because `tvgw-apply` wrote only the global `ip_forward=1`, which the kernel copies to interfaces only when the value changes. The script now writes the per-interface switches for the TV interface and the uplink. Found by `tvgw verify` during the first deploy; see `docs/incidents.md`.
- New rules: invalid conntrack states dropped; GRE, ESP and 6in4 dropped; ICMP tunnels dropped (small pings allowed); DoQ alternate ports 784 and 8853 rejected; TV DNS aimed at other resolvers counted apart from DNS to the gateway; the TV may talk to the gateway address only; nothing unsolicited from the VPN side; the Pi's NetBIOS and SSDP chatter kept off the TV subnet; IPv6 dropped at the wire; LG's hardcoded firmware-fallback address logged.
- Idle TCP flows are reported after 2h04m instead of five days. `nftables.service` is masked so a `flush ruleset` can never wipe the rules.

### `tvgw verify`

- New command and a 10-minute timer (`tvgw verify --record`). Checks the ruleset and that it is unchanged since apply, forwarding, IPv6, the TV's route and policy rule, the VPN's DNAT counters, the DoH set, resolver rewrites and must-allow names, and the services. Stores the verdict and a snapshot of every firewall counter.

### Brief and store

- Red lines at the top: a must-allow name blocked, a clock host blocked or never looked up while the TV was on, failed `verify` checks, ingest warnings.
- New sections: coverage with `NOT ASSESSED`, firewall counters ("Firewall held"), clock hosts, traffic while the TV was off, private reverse lookups, LAN probes, unresolved flows, firmware changes with the names that followed, DNS oddities, allowed-but-unexpected names by category, and a `new domain` marker on new hosts.

### Profile
